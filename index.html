Three stories from the field

Three engagements, and what actually changed for the team on the other side.

~700

Picnic — new hires per year, live on day one

The problem
High-volume hiring meant hundreds of new starters a year, and too many of them hit a login failure on their first morning. Every failure became a manual IT ticket on the day the person was least able to wait.
What I did
Built zero-touch provisioning from Workday into the identity stack, so account creation, group membership and system access were driven by the hire event itself rather than by a follow-up request.
What changed
Day-1 system access for roughly 700 hires a year, with onboarding login failures eliminated and the ticket queue that came with them gone.

CSRD

Munters — audit-ready workforce reporting

The problem
New CSRD sustainability reporting obligations required workforce figures that were consistent, traceable and defensible to an auditor — not assembled by hand each cycle.
What I did
Built the Workday reporting and data-extraction tooling behind the workforce disclosures, with the calculation logic held in the tenant so the numbers could be reproduced on demand.
What changed
Workforce data published in the Munters Annual & Sustainability Report 2025, and a repeatable process for every reporting cycle that follows.

5–15 hrs

Barings — manual processing saved every week

The problem
HR and Finance were carrying recurring manual processing — rekeying, reconciling and chasing data between Workday and the systems around it, every single week.
What I did
Replaced the manual steps with integrations and scheduled automation, and reworked the surrounding business processes so the exceptions became visible instead of invisible.
What changed
Between 5 and 15 hours a week of manual processing returned to the HR and Finance teams, with fewer errors reaching downstream systems.

Questions people ask before we start

How do you price an engagement?

Most work runs on a day rate, quoted on request and fixed for the length of the assignment. For clearly bounded deliverables — a single integration build, an architecture review, a tenant health check — I also quote a fixed price so you know the total up front. No agency margin sits on top: you contract with RXD Cloud Consulting directly.

Can you work through my agency or payroll intermediary?

Yes. I contract directly with end clients and equally through recruitment agencies, consultancies and payroll intermediaries. If your procurement process requires a preferred supplier, send me their details and I'll handle the paperwork with them.

RXD Cloud Consulting is a registered Dutch business (KVK 99369494, BTW NL005382072B24) with multiple clients, its own tools and equipment, and its own professional insurance. How quickly can you start, and what's the minimum engagement?

Typically two to four weeks from a signed contract, sometimes sooner for short scoped work. The minimum is roughly two weeks for a focused deliverable such as an integration build or an architecture review; longer interim assignments usually run three to twelve months.

Do you work remotely, and in which time zones?

I work remotely worldwide from the Netherlands (CET/CEST), in English. I've delivered for teams across Europe, the United States and Asia-Pacific, and will shift hours to hold a workable overlap with yours. On-site visits in the Netherlands and nearby Europe are possible for kick-offs, workshops and go-lives.

What do you need from us to get going?

A tenant with an implementer account, a named business contact who owns the process being automated, and access to the systems on the other side of the integration. If the requirements aren't written down yet, that's fine — scoping them is usually the first piece of work.
